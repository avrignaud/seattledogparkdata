Part II · Access

Share of Seattleites within a 10-minute walk of a legal off-leash area.

99% of Seattleites live that close to any park (TPL). 76.6% live within SPR's published 2.5-mile OLA standard. The gap between "a park" and "a park where dogs are legal" is what this report measures.

Andre Vrignaud · Network-distance analysis · Updated April 23, 2026

Finding 01Any park vs. a legally usable park.

The 11.7% number replaces an earlier straight-line estimate (~33%) with a proper network-distance analysis: every Seattle address, routed along the OSM pedestrian network, to the nearest OLA entrance, with a 10-minute cap at a 3 mph walking pace.

Observation

A properly network-routed walkshed produces a smaller number than a straight-line buffer. Seattle's hills, I-5, and the Ship Canal all cut off routes that "look" close on a map. 11.7% is a ceiling, not a floor.

Finding 02Mapping the OLA gap.

Fourteen fully-fenced off-leash areas, mapped with their half-mile walkshed. The visible gaps — Queen Anne, central Capitol Hill, the western half of West Seattle, most of the Rainier Valley — are where the 88.3% of Seattleites without legal 10-minute access actually live.

Case study · 20 years of documented incidents

Kinnear Park.

Queen Anne's only nearby OLA is the smallest in Seattle — a fenced pen at the back of a steep wooded park. It has been the site of documented dog–dog, dog–human, and neighborhood enforcement incidents for two decades.

Why it matters

A case study of what "OLA" means when the OLA is too small to matter.

Kinnear serves a ZIP code with one of the highest dog-owning household rates in Seattle and no other OLA within reasonable walking distance. At 0.1 acres it sits at roughly one-tenth the AKC-recommended minimum fenced area, and roughly 1/80th the per-capita space benchmark from peer cities.

The result, visible in Seattle Animal Control's citation data, is predictable: Kinnear's ZIP shows disproportionately high off-leash citation density in parks other than Kinnear. When the legal option is too small to use, the use moves elsewhere.
