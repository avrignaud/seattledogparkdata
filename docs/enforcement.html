Observation

Citation hotspots and existing OLA locations are mostly in different places. Discovery, Volunteer, Cal Anderson, Lincoln, Martha Washington, Wallingford Playfield, Seward, and Maple Leaf Reservoir all appear in the top 20 and each has no dedicated OLA. The pocket OLAs (Kinnear, Plymouth Pillars, Regrade, Denny, Magnolia Manor — all below the AKC one-acre minimum) show as isolated sage dots with few or no adjacent hotspots. Magnuson and Westcrest — the two OLAs above eight acres — appear as hotspots because the non-OLA portions of those host parks are regularly used off-leash. Citations cluster where OLAs aren't, or where the OLA that exists is small enough that users seek adjacent open space.

Observation

Citation density concentrates outside OLA walksheds. North of the Ship Canal — Wallingford, Ravenna, Maple Leaf, Green Lake, Laurelhurst — shows heavy citation density with little walkable OLA coverage. Capitol Hill, bounded by Volunteer and Cal Anderson, has two top-10 citation locations and a single 0.2-acre pocket OLA (Plymouth Pillars). The Queen Anne / Magnolia core has Kinnear (0.124 acre) and Magnolia Manor (0.48 acre) — both below the AKC one-acre minimum — and a citation band through Discovery, West Queen Anne Playfield, David Rodgers Park, and Smith Cove. Lincoln Park (West Seattle) carries 122 citations; the nearest OLA, Westcrest, is 3.4 miles away.

Finding 04Citation severity mix.

Seattle Municipal Code 18.12.080(A) escalates citations by offense count — 1st offense is a warning or $54 fine; 2nd is $109; 3rd is $136; 4th and beyond is $162. The majority are first offenses.

Observation

Across the six-year PRR window, the program generated roughly $241,000 in assessed fee revenue against roughly $915,000 in FAS-side ACO salary and divisional overhead. Including the paired SPR-side Facilities Maintenance Worker on the cost side (the 2021 MOA treats ACO + FMW as a team) brings the cost estimate to about $1.76M, taking cost recovery to roughly 14%. Seattle's Parking Enforcement Officers issued roughly 300,000+ tickets in 2019 at an average of $47 each, a revenue-positive program. The off-leash enforcement program is not self-funding at any of the fee tiers currently in place; discussion of policy implications is on the opinion page.

What "confidently folded" means. SPR's internal system recorded the same park under many different strings — inconsistent capitalization, abbreviations, sub-locations appended, minor typos. We collapse these to canonical names using a set of regular expressions in the build script. Every mapping is reviewable in CANONICAL_PARKS. For example:

  • Magnuson Park (248 citations) collapsed from 6 distinct raw strings, including "Warren G Magnuson Park", "Warren G. Magnuson Park", "Magnuson park", "Warren G Magnuson Park - Athletic Fields", and more.
  • Alki Beach Park (55 citations) collapsed from 6 variants, including "Alki Beach @ 52nd Ave SW" and "Alki Beach near 53rd Av SW".
  • Woodland Park (222 citations) collapsed from 5 variants including "Lower Woodland Park ball field" and "Woodland Park Off-Leash". This fold is intentionally lossy: the Lower Woodland OLA (0.75 ac) is inside the larger Woodland Park, and the raw records often do not distinguish citations inside the OLA from citations elsewhere in the park. Citations issued inside a designated OLA are unusual but not impossible (leash-required perimeter, small-dog-area boundaries). The partial-OLA classification on the hotspot map reflects this ambiguity.
  • West Queen Anne Playfield (77 citations) collapsed from 3 variants: "W. Queen Anne Playfield", "West Queen Anne Playfield", "W Queen Anne Playfield".

Across the 281 distinct canonical entries, 43 were multi-variant (i.e. required reconciliation). The remaining 238 distinct park names came through with only one spelling in the raw data.

The 672 street-address rows (14.0%) are now geocoded via the Census Bureau batch geocoder with a Nominatim fallback in scripts/geocode_street_addresses.py — 457 of 481 unique addresses matched (95.0%). Those 645 citations now feed the combined walkshed-overlap pass on Part II Finding 02b. The 111 blank-location rows remain unplaceable — they still count in aggregate totals and the offense-level breakdown, but not on the map.

Combined, citations with blank or street-only locations total 783 (16.3%). This is the floor on spatial analysis precision. The park-level totals and the hotspot map should be read as covering the 4,020 citations (83.7%) with an identifiable park name, not the full 4,803.

Other caveats

Coordinates. OLA host-park coordinates are reconciled to SPR's Dog Off-Leash Areas ArcGIS FeatureServer (April 2026 pull); non-host parks use hand-geocoded approximations from park names in data/park-coordinates.csv. Accurate enough for a city-scale map, not for legal or engineering use. Canonical geometry for Seattle parks is on Seattle's ArcGIS Open Data portal.

"No OLA" classification. A park is labeled "no OLA" if it contains no fenced, dedicated off-leash area. Large parks with a small OLA in one section (Woodland Park, containing the 0.75-acre Lower Woodland OLA) are labeled "partial" because citations in the non-OLA portions of the park are still illegal off-leash use.

2019 is partial. The dataset ends October 15, 2019. Year-over-year comparisons involving 2019 need to be interpreted as partial-year. A follow-up public records request has been submitted for October 2019 through present and is awaiting response (request text at prrs/01-spr-offleash-citations-post-2019.md).

What this data does and doesn't say. Citations reflect enforcement activity, not underlying violation rates. A drop in citations at a given park could mean fewer violations, fewer patrols, or both. A concentration at a given park reflects both where violations occur and where rangers chose to patrol. SPR deployment patterns over 2014–2019 are not in this dataset; a separate PRR would be needed.

Fee column. Recorded fees in the raw data are $0 (1st-offense verbal warning), $54 (1st-offense citation), $109 (2nd), $136 (3rd), $162 (4th+). The Seattle Municipal Code 18.12.080(A) and fine schedule are the legal reference.
