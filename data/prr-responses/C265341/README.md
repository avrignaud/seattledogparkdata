# PRR C265341 — Current SPR/FAS Animal Control Officer MOA + 2022 SLI response

## Request

Sent by Andre Vrignaud via the Seattle Public Records Request Center
(**C265341-042926**). Requested the inter-departmental agreements and expenditure
authorizations under which SPR is expanding Animal Control Officer (ACO) coverage for
park-based dog enforcement — the documents that supersede/amend the 2016 MOA and the
signed 2021 MOA (AG21-PRF03-032). Specifically: (1) the current MOU/MOA, (2) its
Attachment A cost basis, (3) total authorized FTE and annual cost, (4) the 2025–2026
budget line items, and (5) any planning memos or council presentations.

This is the FAS response that fulfilled the SPR/FAS half of what the site tracks as
[PRR #7](../../../prrs/07-spr-fas-aco-staffing-expansion.md) (originally filed as
C264119-042026; the City Budget Office closed its portion with no responsive records).

## Key findings

### The current MOA (items #1–4)

Signed **2023-04-18** (FAS, Acting Director Kiersten Grove) and **2023-04-27** (SPR,
Interim Superintendent AP Diaz). Successor to AG21-PRF03-032. Term runs through
**2027-12-31**.

- **Staffing:** funds **three ACO II positions** (the prior single ACO plus **two new
  positions added by the Seattle Park District Board for 2023**), each paired with an SPR
  **Facilities Maintenance Worker (FMW)** — SPR's reclassified Park Ranger role.
- **Schedule:** Wednesday–Saturday or Sunday–Wednesday (two overlapping teams = 7-day
  coverage); 1000–2000 during DST, 0700–1700 otherwise.
- **Cost (Attachment A / item #2):** per FTE — $44.79/hr (top 2022 rate; 2023 AWI then in
  negotiation) × 2,088 hrs = $93,522 salary, + 47.97% benefits = $44,864, + $3,000
  supplies + $10,165 divisional indirect = **$151,551/FTE**. **3-FTE total $454,652/yr**
  FAS-side. (Per-FTE is essentially flat vs. the 2021 MOA's $152,399.) Overtime not
  included unless pre-authorized.
- **Budget codes (item #4):** SPR (paying) — Project **PRF1602** / Activity OPS / Org
  FAI01 / Fund 10200 / PCBU PR0 / Account 541900. FAS (billing) — Project
  **FA4MSSASENF** / Activity PARKS / Org FAI01 / Fund 00100 / PCBU FA4 / Account 360750.
  Funded under "Making Parks Safer," a sub-element of the Park District's Maintaining
  Parks & Facilities initiative.
- **Reporting (§5):** SPR must give FAS a **monthly summary report** of parks-patrol data
  with each invoice; **§7** references a per-period cost calculation spreadsheet. Neither
  is in this response — both are public-records-eligible and would resolve the
  *funded-vs-actually-filled* FTE question and real per-park parks-patrol activity.

### The 2022 SLI response (item #5)

FAS's September 30, 2022 report to Council on SLI **FAS-003-B-001** ("Increased
Efficiencies in Animal Control Patrolling," sponsored by CM Lisa Herbold). Origin: a
2022 budget proviso would have **restricted $194,000** to fund two ACOs dedicated solely
to parks; FAS argued for flexibility and it became a study (the SLI). New citable data:

- **SAS 2022 staffing matrix:** ACO I 8.0 approved / 5.0 filled; ACO II 14.0 approved /
  12.0 filled; **2.0 FTE on parks patrols**; 7-day operation.
- **2021 calls for service (FSU): 5,596 total** — Parks Patrols **1,850 (33%**, second
  largest), field service 1,944 (35%), cruelty 741 (13%), aggressive-animal 534 (10%),
  wildlife 527 (9%). Top five field calls: noise 461, leash-law 364, confined stray 310,
  vet transport 298, DOA 139.
- **Coverage:** ACO IIs patrol parks in pairs, 80 hrs/week; in 2021 they patrolled **344
  of 489 parks (70%)**.
- **Top-20 parks by calls for service (7/2021–7/2022):** Volunteer (61), Judkins (33),
  Alki Beach (33), Lincoln Park (26), W. Queen Anne Playfield (25), Cal Anderson (23),
  Magnuson (21), Rogers Playground (20), Madison Middle School (20), Interbay (19),
  Discovery (18), Maple Leaf Reservoir (17), Loyal Heights (16), TT Minor (16),
  Wallingford (16), Meridian (14), Golden Gardens (13), Soundview (12), Salmon Bay (12),
  Sandel (12). A calls-for-service ranking — distinct from, and cross-validating, the
  site's citation-based hotspots.
- **The authority gap (pp. 3–4):** ACO IIs are not police. Their special commission lets
  them issue citations, serve warrants, testify, pull state licensing data, and enter
  posted property — but **cannot compel identification from a refuser, and cannot
  detain**. ID is required to issue an infraction. FAS: SPD "have historically assisted…
  but recently… have been unable to assist," so officers now "issue verbal warnings on
  all first contacts." Residents "regularly ignore officers' requests for
  identification or simply walk away or flee" and use Facebook groups to share patrol
  locations and encourage non-compliance (Attachment B is a screenshot; Attachment A is
  an Alki patrol log: 6 off-leash contacts, all refused ID, 0 citations). This is a
  city-documented mechanism by which citation output can fall while patrols continue.
- **FAS on record** endorsing OLA expansion as a compliance tool: increasing
  "opportunities to comply with the law, like adding and promoting Off Leash Areas… will
  help us achieve better compliance." Also documents environmental harms (fecal coliform,
  ground-nesting bird habitat, marine-mammal danger at Alki) and counts "15 Off Leash
  Areas… with three more coming online" (a department's loose 2022 count, including City
  Light property; the site enumerates 14).

## Why this PRR matters for the site

It is the cost-and-authority backbone of the enforcement story: it documents that the
park-patrol program **tripled its funded ACO headcount in 2023** (one → three) at
**$454,652/yr** FAS-side under traceable Park District budget codes, and — via the SLI
response — that the enforcement itself is **structurally unable to cite a person who
refuses to identify themselves**. See [Enforcement page](../../../docs/enforcement.html)
(authority-gap note under Finding 02; cost footnote) and [Opinion O1](../../../docs/opinion.html#O1).
