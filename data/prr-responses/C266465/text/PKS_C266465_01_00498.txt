Potential Elevator Pitch:

The partnership between SPR and Animal Control is critical to promoting compliance with
leash and scoop laws at our park locations, especially those with a history of user complaints
and violations that impact both the experience of park visitors and the health of our natural
resources. Historically, a FWM partnered with ACOs for regular patrols aimed at encouraging
responsible dog ownership and behavior. With the growth of the Park Ranger program, SPR
has transitioned to utilizing Park Rangers on a limited basis in this effort, but it is clear that
Animal Control's expertise and consistent presence remain essential to achieving the
program's goals due to their database of contacts and experience enforcing dog violations.
To continue improving park safety and user experience, we recommend increasing reliance
on Animal Control to conduct regular patrols and enforce compliance, while also investing in




                                                                                        PKS C266465 01 00501
training and access to the ACO database to increase Park Rangers ability to also do this,
ensuring a safer and more enjoyable environment for all park users from both agencies.

Currently, Park Rangers are only able to partner with ACOs Sunday through Wednesday, and
only when a single ACO is available without a partner. To improve coverage and maintain a
consistent presence, it would be beneficial to have two ACOs assigned to the park patrols:
one for Sunday through Wednesday, and another for Wednesday through Saturday, rather
than the two FTE's working only Sunday-Wednesday. This schedule would ensure patrols are
conducted seven days a week, with one Park Ranger available each day to collaborate with
the ACO. This alignment would strengthen our efforts to enforce park regulations and
enhance overall park safety.

A regular reporting mechanism will also be vital for tracking progress and outcomes.
Monthly reports from ACOs should include park complaints received, the number of
warnings and citations issued, and the number of patrols completed by ACOs, both with and
without Park Rangers. In support of this effort, SPR can assist by informing the public of the
increased enforcement through social media, posting clearer signage throughout all parks
regarding the Parks Code (particularly around dog-related violations), and improving the
data tracking and incident reporting system. We hope the in-progress GIS app will help
streamline and improve these systems for both agencies.
