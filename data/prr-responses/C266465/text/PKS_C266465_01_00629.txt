              Dogs and dog parks - the reality of Seattle Parks and Recreation Off-leash areas

In 2011, the number of dogs in the city of Seattle was greater than the number of children: 153,000 dogs to 107,000
children. That's about 1.4 dogs for every child. Nationwide, that number is 1.1 to l.1 There are many reasons to own a
dog, ranging from our own health to benefits to the community. In order to allow dogs to be safely off-leash, they need
designated areas to protect other dogs, protect the land, and protect the people around them. But it is difficult to
create dog off-leash areas due to funding, perceptions of public health and safety and the specialized use of these areas.

Benefits to dog ownership:
     •     Health:       Owners are more likely to have a strong heart and lower cholesterol, and less likely to report feeling
           lonely
     •     Community Benefits:               dog parks and dog-friendly walk routes can help reduce crime because of the extra eyes
           and ears on the street. Frequent dog-walkers in any given neighborhood tend to know each other.
     •     Off-leash areas (OLA) are gathering places for dog owners and dogs. OLA's are sometimes considered the
           playgrounds for families of the K9 community. Citizens for Off Leash Areas (COLA) says that we need places like
           these that foster community in our neighborhoods.2

Dogs are welcome in most parks:
           Fourteen off-leash areas are listed on the Seattle Parks and Recreation website. Just outside of the city, there
           are also exceptional dog parks at Mercer Island's Luther Burbank Park and Redmond's Marymoor Park, which
           has forty acres of off-leash area.3

           Off leash areas were created on 1995, but the same problem exists 20 years later. Some oppose new Seattle
           off-leash areas because only 20 percent of dog owners license their pets. Seattle pet-license fees do not go
           toward off-leash area funding.4

           Over the last couple of years, the city developed further plans for dog parks which includes $106,000 to fund
           maintenance and improvement of existing dog parks. The parks department also wants to support efforts of
           groups like COLA to get funding to create more off-leash dog parks, and to help start a public engagement
           process to develop 3-4 pilot parks. The council was advised that possible sites are Hubbard Homestead Park,
           Stone Way at 125th Street, Maple Leaf Reservoir, and Adams Street Boat Ramp. Arguments to restrict and place
           limitations on Seattle's off-leash dog parks range from public safety to claims the parks devolve into mud
           puddles filled with bacteria. The bacteria is from dog feces which gets washed into area waters.

Recent recommendations to the City Council for future dog parks and regulations:
         • Licensed dogs should be allowed on-leash in all Seattle parks, except for children's playgrounds and ball fields.
         • Incrementally increase the number of Seattle off-leash dog parks after a community engagement process.
         • Support groups like COLA creating dog parks off of public land (ie. Private dog off leash areas).
         • Encourage private developers to create their own off-leash parks.
         • Only promote fenced off-leash parks on city land.
         • Do not promote leash-optional trails.
         • Require dog walkers to become certified and licensed in Seattle.
