Key Policy Issues:

      1. Process for Adding New OLAs
         SPR recognizes off-leash areas as a valid use of park land and is committed to working with
         communities and volunteer groups to identify and develop new OLAs. Our process for identifying new
         locations will be based on community interest; we will not identify pre-determined locations for
         future OLAs. SPR supports the addition of new OLAs in several ways, as described below.


         New OLAs may be added in the following ways:

         a. Through new park development or existing park redevelopment processes. Land-banked sites will
            be examined for suitability as OLAs as they are developed.

         b. Through requests by communities, whether through Neighborhood Matching Fund processes or
            other community engagement processes. Once identified, proposed OLAs would be reviewed by
            an OLA Review Committee as outlined in Key Policy Issue #5 below.

         c. By supporting groups like COLA in developing new OLAs on non-park public land suitable for OLAs,
            by SPR convening the OLA Review Committee, as described below, assisting with design, and

2.   Fenced vs Unfenced Off Leash Areas


     a. SPR is committed to continue to offer only fenced OLAs due to the potential for conflict between
        leashed and unleashed dogs and between dogs and other park users, very limited enforcement
        resources, maintenance issues, and feedback from other jurisdictions that have implemented
        unfenced OLAs and are now moving away from unfenced dog parks. SPR is particularly concerned
        about the public safety of park users who may be co-users of these spaces.

     b. SPR does not recommend designated leash-optional trails due to reasons listed above. In addition
        to maintenance and environmental concerns, forest stewards report frequent damage to newly
        planted native plants by off leash dogs.

     Park Board: The Board of Park Commissioners supports the SPR position to only have fenced OLAs on
     park property.


        Does the Mayor concur with having only fenced off leash areas and no leash-optional
        trails?


 3. Park District Funding
    SPR proposes using Park District funding (approximately $106,000/year through 2020) to make capital
    improvements to the existing OLAs based on the facility assessments included in the Draft Plan. SPR
    is committed to working closely with COLA, and local community groups on some of the specific
    improvements at existing OLAs to ensure our assessments and planned improvements are aligned
    with users’ experience, with the caveat that improvements must meet SPR’s construction and
    maintenance specifications as well as all applicable codes.

     Capital costs for construction of new OLAs vary widely depending on the specific site so accurate
     estimates of costs per OLA are difficult. To aid in planning, SPR developed a planning-level cost
     estimate for generic new one-acre OLAs. The estimate includes parking and ADA access, drainage,
     grading and other site work and a double-gated fence; it also includes costs for the kinds of
     amenities COLA is requesting such as separated large and small dog areas, benches, dog play
     equipment and protected landscaping. Including taxes, permits, and project management costs,
     construction of a new one-acre OLA is estimated at $966,000. For smaller sites or few amenities, the

4. Dog Walker License, Certification and Limits

  a. SPR’s Plan proposes a requirement for professional dog walkers using OLAs to become licensed
     ($100/year).

     Marymoor Park in King County has a 40-acre OLA. King County charges dog walkers $800/year to
     walk ten dogs five days per week. The fee may be reduced if a dog walker walks fewer days per
     week and/or volunteers to clean dog waste during non-dog walking hours. For example, a dog
     walker who walks dogs two days per week would be charged $400. Dog walkers are credited
     $20/hour for working in the Marymoor OLA. The $800 King County fee includes a $98 dog walker
     base license fee and a $702 impact fee.

     SPR has coordinated with FAS and Animal Control during the development of this proposal.
     Originally, FAS intended to administer the dog walker license, but changed their minds since it
     only applies to Park OLA’s. Instead, the license will be proposed as an amendment to the Park
     Code and administered by SPR permitting staff. The license requirement will be enforced by
     Animal Control which is now staffed with two Animal Control Officers who are paired with two
     SPR staff in their park enforcement work.

     To ensure safety of all OLA users, for a two-year transition period, dog walkers will be allowed to
     bring 10 licensed dogs into OLAs. At the end of two years, dog walkers who have obtained a
     three-course certification from the University of Washington or another equivalent program
     authorized by the Superintendent, will be allowed to continue to bring up to 10 licensed dogs
     into OLAs. Dog walkers who have not received certification will be restricted to bringing 3 dogs
     into OLAs or parks. Criteria for any authorized equivalent dog-walker certification programs are
     being finalized and will be included in the final plan. Several vendors of such programs have
     expressed interest in submitting their training programs for approval by the Superintendent.

  b. Originally, SPR recommended an increase in fines for repeat violators at the suggestion of Animal
     Control Officers. We learned subsequently that proposed changes to the Animal Code were
     delayed. SPR will revisit this issue with FAS when and if the proposed revisions go forward.

      5. OLA Review Committee

       For each proposed new OLA, SPR will convene an ad-hoc committee including dog advocates,
       environmental experts, a veterinarian or animal behaviorist, community members and SPR staff. This
       committee will consider the proposal and will make a recommendation to the Superintendent. SPR
       will maintain a list of approved OLAs for development when funding is available.

       Park Board: The Park Board supported this process and urged SPR to consider underserved areas,
       notably SE Seattle and the northern end of West Seattle, in determining the order of approved
       projects to be developed when funding is available. While there is no “standard” for development of
       new OLAs, only two areas of Seattle do not have an OLA within two miles: the north end of West
       Seattle and Southeast Seattle.

       SPR concurs with this advice on ranking projects for development and will develop a set of equity
       criteria when prioritizing funding determinations.


         Does the Mayor support having a committee, as described above, to make
         recommendations on new OLAs?



Background:

The Draft People, Dogs and Parks Plan was developed in response to a 2014 City Council Statement of Legislative
Intent, amended to allow funding from the Seattle Park District to provide resources for developing the Draft
Plan. The Council requested development of a long-term plan for the City’s existing 14 off-leash areas (OLAs),
and for maintenance, acquisition and expansion of OLA projects. A previous briefing for the Mayor’s Office on the
off-leash area plan occurred in February 2016.

The Board of Park Commissioners considered the Draft Plan at several meetings in 2016; the Board held a public
hearing on the Draft Plan on December 8, 2016 and unanimously recommended approval of the Plan at their
January 12, 2017 meeting.

Indicate Legislation and/or Council action

The City Council Parks, Seattle Center, Libraries and Waterfront Committee held a meeting to discuss the Plan on
December 8, 2016. Committee members requested a detailed implementation plan which will be included in the
final Plan. Two Councilmembers expressed interest in a pilot unfenced off leash area proposal, adding that they
may require SPR to develop a report on unfenced off-leash areas. All other questions were answered at the
meeting.

There is no action required by the City Council on the Plan itself, but there would be legislation required to
create a fee for dog walkers and for any increases in fees for off leash violations.


Please give a brief overview of any financial impacts

The Seattle Park District’s 6-year financial plan includes approximately $106,000 annually through 2020 for off-
leash areas. The Draft Plan includes initial priorities for spending these funds on improvements to the existing 14
OLAs. The priorities will be further refined following further discussions with COLA. COLA submitted an initial list
of improvements in 2015 which were reviewed and included, as appropriate, in the capital improvement list.
COLA recently submitted another list which SPR is reviewing with them. SPR anticipates finalizing the capital
improvement list for 2017 and 2018 for inclusion in the Final Plan.

              Attachment 1: Changes Proposed for the Final People, Dogs and Parks Plan


SPR proposes to modify the Draft Plan in the following ways.

   1.   Clarify language in the draft plan to more clearly express departmental support for off-leash areas.

   2.   Modify language in the plan to be clarify that SPR is fully committed to taking an enterprising
        approach to finding ways to site OLAs.

   3.   Add COLA appendix that outlines potential new OLA sites to Plan, with stipulation that any proposed
        OLA must be reviewed by the community review processes described below.

   4.   Designate a staff person to be the department lead on off-leash areas and serve as liaison with COLA,
        Magnuson Off Leash Group (MOLG) and other interested residents.

   5.   Support the concept of pilot sites with the understanding that we can support only fenced areas. All
        OLAs are initially “pilot sites.”

   6.    Evaluate proposals for pilot sites through our normal community engagement process, including with
        the ad-hoc committee described in the draft plan. Work closely with COLA, neighborhood residents
        and environmental experts once these sites have been identified.

   7.   Maintain off leash areas on non-Park, publically-owned land assuming we are funded to do so.

   8.   Support efforts by COLA and MOLG to engage in fundraising and grant writing for off-leash areas.

   9.   Include a public engagement process in the development of 3 or 4 pilot sites, per the community
        engagement process outlined in the draft strategic plan: Hubbard Homestead Park, Stone Way at
        125th, Maple Leaf Reservoir and Adams Street Boat Ramp.

   10. Review plans for OLA improvements with COLA.

   11. Understand that COLA want more OLAs with water access. Agree that more work is needed to
      review regulatory requirements and develop standards for water access, including public health
      standards, accessibility, fencing, safety, etc. In nearly all cases where shoreline access is considered,
      we would need approval from a regulatory agency.

   12. Recommend regular meetings/check-ins between the department and COLA and MOLG.
