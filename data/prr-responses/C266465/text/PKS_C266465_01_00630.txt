At Little Brook Park       Since the Array Apartments (14027 Lake City Way) was completed and began renting to residents in April of
         2013, reports from area residents are that issues with dog owners in LB Park have increased significantly. Most issues are for
         those leaving pet waste on the grassy area (about half of the park area). LBP has improved in recent years. A decade ago, drug
         dealers, loud teens, and outdoor (or bathroom) sex were the main park uses.

         In summer-time, the needs and amenities of the park ramp up considerably. The park is the only public park space for 1 - 2
         miles. The closest Seattle off-leash area is nearly 3 miles away, on the west side of the 1-5 freeway. Shoreline access is better.
         An off-leash area there is only 0.8 miles away (driving distance is still closer to 1 mile).

Community conflict         Dog owners allow their dogs to leave waste piles where children play. The children and families have no
         other place to play yet the dogs can still play in a waste-filled park space. Why do dog owners continue to act with (what
         may be perceived to be) contempt toward the rest of the LBP-using community?

         •    Dog walkers consider that the park is quite convenient to their needs (especially if they are coming from Array). This
              may over-ride concern for others.
         •    They may be unaware of the impact they are making.
         •    They may also be frustrated by the limits place on them by available space to run and walk dogs.
         •    They may subconsciously conflate the privilege they have to share their apartment with their dog (plus promised
              proximity to pet supply stores and veterinary clinics) with a perception that Little Brook park is part of the package of
              amenities that comes in their rental.
         •    They also pay extra for their pet at the Array apartments: They pay a pet deposit of $250 and $20 per pet monthly pet
              rent. That payment could emphasize the sense of entitlement to use the park as they like.
