                                  MEMORANDUM OF AGREEMENT
                                            BETWEEN
                                SEATTLE PARKS AND RECREATION AND
                 SEATTLE DEPARTMENT OF FINANCE AND ADMINISTRATIVE SERVICES FOR
                        STAFFING TO SUPPORT "MAKING PARKS SAFER" Program



1.   PURPOSE
This Memorandum of Agreement ("Agreement") is made by and between Seattle Parks and Recreation
("SPR") and the Department of Finance and Administrative Services ("FAS"), representing its Seattle Animal
Shelter ("SAS"). This agreement supports the work of the Making Parks Safer program, funded by the Seattle
Park District ("Park District"). The agreement addresses scope of work, schedule and conditions of work,
reporting and compensation and billing.


This Agreement is for the funding of up to three (3) ACO II ("ACO") full time equivalents (FTE) employed by
SAS. The agreement will fund up to three (3) FTE for a combined maximum of 6,264 (2,088 x 3) hours
annually which can be performed with any number of positions working up to the maximum allowable
hours. The ACO will work in partnership with SPR staff, primarily Park Rangers. The team's work program is
to educate the public and enforce leash and scoop laws at SPR park locations, especially those locations
with a history of complaints, violations and impacts to users and/or natural resources based on park users'
non-compliance with these laws.


2.   SCOPE OF WORK
While working as a team, the ACO, in partnership with an SPR Park Ranger, will seek to educate park users
and promote compliance with all applicable animal control laws in SPR parks. Enforcement of these laws is
within the purview of SAS, and the ACO will have the discretionary authority to issue citations for non-
compliance. It is anticipated, however, that the work program will be primarily educational, and that park
users' observation of the ACO/Park Ranger team will have a deterrent effect in terms of non-compliance.
The partnership between SPR and ACO is critical to promoting compliance with leash and scoop laws at our
park locations, especially those with a history of user complaints and violations that impact both the
experience of park visitors and the health of our natural resources. To continue improving park safety and
user experience, we recommend increasing reliance on SAS to conduct regular patrols and enforce
compliance, while also investing in training and access to the ACO database to increase Park Rangers' ability
to also do this, ensuring a safer and more enjoyable environment for all park users from both agencies. The
ACO may also carry out FAS/SAS-identified, or ad hoc service tasks as assigned by the SAS leadership team;
and other duties as assigned. SPR and FAS will establish guidelines by which they will create the team's
work itineraries and the degree to which these itineraries will remain flexible, for instance to respond to
emergent issues and/or complaints.


3.   WORK SCHEDULE
The typical work schedule described in this Agreement will take place on a Wednesday through Saturday or
Sunday through Wednesday schedule. Work hours will vary by season and programmatically, based on high-
use times and to maximize the number of daylight hours the team can spend in parks. The typical hours will
be 9:00 AM to 7:00 PM during daylight savings time and 7:00 AM to 5:00 PM when daylight savings end. The
team may also need to flex their hours to attend community meetings to discuss park-related topics.

The ACO and Park Ranger will either report to or meet at the SAS and then depart for the day's planned
itinerary or alternatively, by mutual agreement, at a SPR maintenance facility in a location determined to
suit the needs of the program. The Park Ranger and ACO will share driving responsibilities of both FAS and
SPR vehicles and each program team member shall have a key for the vehicle being used that day.


Repairs of damage to department vehicles will be the responsibility of the department of the driver when
the damage occurred. The ACO and the Park Ranger will spend most of their shift engaged in the work
described in this Agreement, including the time spent to travel to and between parks and to return to either
SAS or other designated location at the end of their shift. An exception will be for department or program
required trainings, meetings and other designated events.


4.   CONDITIONS OF WORK
While in a park, the Park Ranger and ACO will always maintain radio communication with each other with
an emphasis to keep a line-of-sight contact at all times and be ready to move into close physical proximity
should an interaction with a park user become charged. The Park Ranger and the ACO should view
themselves as a team, working together and communicating with each other to address issues of concern to
both SPR and FAS. The ACO shall be responsible for organizing the daily patrol area and route based on
complaints received by SAS. Other duties performed by this team shall be shared. SAS will process and hold
all law enforcement agency copies of citations issued by ACOs for the required retention period. SPR will
process and hold all law enforcement agency copies of citations issued by Park Ranger for the required
retention period. SPR will be responsible for supplying the Park Ranger with a citation book from SPD. The
ACO will carry barrier equipment, which may be used as a tool or barrier to push away an aggressive animal.
The Park Ranger will carry similar protective equipment. The ACO and Park Ranger will both carry cell
phones as well as any other equipment mutually agreed to by SPR and FAS.


5.   DOCUMENTATION AND REPORTING
SPR will provide the Park Ranger with the electronic equipment needed to record the origin and type of
interaction with a member of the public, the infraction observed, and the result of the interaction, (i.e.,
patrol and incident; scoop violation; warning). SPR will also collect data on the location and time of each
interaction and on each visit by the team to a single park. SPR may also collect other points of data that
reasonably relate to the work program and its goals. The Park Ranger will enter the data into the Park
Ranger Data Tracking App. SPR will share the data collected with FAS. The ACO and Park Ranger shall record
all contacts and citations in the SPR app on the Parks Code Violation Dashboard. FAS will provide access to
PetPoint and training for Park Rangers from ACOs on ticket writing and utilizing PetPoint to track contacts.
A regular reporting mechanism will be vital for tracking progress and outcomes. Monthly reports from ACOs
should include park complaints received, the number of warnings and citations issued, and the number of
patrols completed by ACOs, both with and without Park Rangers.


Within five working days of the end of each month, SPR will provide FAS a summary report on that month's
data, which FAS will include with its invoice to SPR Accounting.

 7.   BILLING AND PAYMENT

 annually up to a total of $329,235 ($109,745 per ACO) in direct salary costs and up to an additional $147,414
 ($49,138 per ACO) for all ACO employee benefits (variable + pooled) based on actual hours worked.
 Overtime is not included unless pre-authorized by SPR. The ACO will remain on the FAS payroll and code
 hours worked to a project code. Timesheets for the Park Ranger will be approved by the SPR supervisor and
 timesheets for the ACO will be approved by the SAS Field Services Supervisor.


 In addition, SPR will reimburse FAS program and supply costs of up to a total of $9,000 ($3,000 per ACO)
 and FAS divisional overhead of up to $23,046 ($7,682 per ACO) based on actual hours worked. SPR will not
