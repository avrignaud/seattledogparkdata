1.   PURPOSE
This Memorandum of Agreement ("Agreement") is made by and between Seattle Parks and Recreation
("SPR") and the Department of Finance and Administrative Services ("FAS"), representing its Seattle Animal
Shelter ("SAS"). This agreement supports the work of the Making Parks Safer program, funded by the
Seattle Park District ("Park District"). The agreement addresses scope of work, schedule and conditions of
work, reporting and compensation and billing.


This Agreement is for the funding, of up to three (3) ACO II ("ACO") full time equivalent (FTE) employed by
SAS. The agreement will fund up to three (3) FTE for a combined maximum of 6,264 (2,088 X 3) hours
annually which can be performed with any number of positions working up the maximum allowable
hours. The ACO will work in partnership with SPR staff, primarily Park Rangers. The team's work program is
to educate the public and enforce - leash and scoop laws at SPR park locations, especially those locations
with a history of complaints, violations and impacts to users and/or natural resources based on park users'
non-compliance with these laws.


2.   SCOPE OF WORK
While working as a team, the ACO, in partnership with an SPR Park Ranger will seek to educate park users
and promote compliance with all applicable animal-control laws in SPR parks. Enforcement of these laws
is within the purview of SAS, and the ACO will have the discretionary authority to issue citations for non-
compliance. It is anticipated, however, that the work program will be primarily educational, and that park
users' observation of the ACO/Park Ranger team will have a deterrent effect in terms of non-compliance.
The partnership between SPR and ACO is critical to promoting compliance with leash and scoop laws at

3.   WORK SCHEDULE
The typical work schedule described in this Agreement will take place on a Wednesday through Saturday

program required trainings, meetings and other designated events.


4.   CONDITIONS OF WORK
While in a park, the Park Ranger and ACO will always maintain radio communication with each other with
an emphasis to keep a line-of-sight contact at all times and be ready to move into close physical proximity
should an interaction with a park user become charged. The Park Ranger and the ACO should view
themselves as a team, working together and communicating with each other to address issues of concern
to both SPR and FAS. The ACO shall be responsible for organizing the daily patrol area and route based on
complaints received by SAS. Other duties performed by this team shall be shared. SAS will process and
hold all law enforcement agency copies of citations issued by ACOs for the required retention period. SPR
will process and hold all law enforcement agency copies of citations issued by Park Ranger for the required
retention period. SPR will be responsible for supplying the Park Ranger with a citation book from SPD. SPR
will provide a copy of citations issued for leash law violations by Park Ranger to SAS for recording in the
SAS database. The ACO will carry barrier equipment, which may be used as a tool or barrier to push away
an aggressive animal. The Park Ranger will carry similar protective equipment. The ACO and Park Ranger
will both carry cell phones as well as any other equipment mutually agreed to by SPR and FAS.


5.   DOCUMENTATION AND REPORTING
SPR will provide the Park Ranger with the electronic equipment needed to record the origin and type of
interaction with a member of the public, the infraction observed, and the result of the interaction, (i.e.,.,
patrol and incident; scoop violation; warning). SPR will also collect data on the location and time of each
interaction and on each visit by the team to a single park. SPR may also collect other points of data that
reasonably relate to the work program and its goals. The Park Ranger will enter the data into the Park
Ranger Data Tracking App. SPR will share the data collected with FAS. The ACO and Park Ranger shall
record all contacts and citations in the SPR app on the Parks Code Violation Dashboard. FAS will provide
access to PetPoint, Training for Park Rangers from ACOs on ticket writing and utilizing PetPoint to track
contacts. A regular reporting mechanism will be vital for tracking progress and outcomes. Monthly
reports from ACOs should include park complaints received, the number of warnings and citations issued,
and the number of patrols completed by ACOs, both with and without Park Rangers.
