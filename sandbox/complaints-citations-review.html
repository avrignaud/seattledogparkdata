Resident complaints vs. enforcement

Three candidate exhibits for the enforcement page, built from the Find It Fix It “Nuisance Dogs in a Park” complaints (PRR C263990, 2024–26) and the Animal Control off-leash citations (PRRs C049204 + C263949). Numbers are live from the analysis pipeline; layout and copy are placeholders for us to mark up.

Fig 1 · Over time

Complaints climbed; citations fell away

Two stacked panels on a shared timeline — deliberately not a dual-axis chart, which would let the scaling imply a correlation that isn’t there. Monthly counts since complaints began (April 2024).

Monthly Find It Fix It complaints

Monthly dog-loose-in-park citations

Source: Find It Fix It “Nuisance Dogs in a Park” (Seattle Open Data 5ngg-rpne, PRR C263990) · Seattle Animal Control off-leash citations (PRRs C049204 + C263949). Dog-loose-in-park citations only.

What it shows Complaints rose ~3.5× from 2024 to 2025 (3,010 for the year) while citations fell ~40% (267) and went nearly silent after mid-2025. Monthly correlation r = 0.13 — no lead or lag in either direction. Animal Control runs ≈1 FTE, so citations can’t scale with complaint demand. The two systems run in parallel, not in sequence.

Fig 2 · Where

Same hotspots, different lens

Each circle is a park (2024–26). Size = total off-leash activity (complaints + citations); color = which system dominates. Orange parks are where neighbors report; navy parks are where officers cite. Small sage rings mark the 14 existing off-leash areas.

What it shows Both systems light up the same off-leash hotspots (rank correlation ≈0.5–0.6). What differs is the ratio: destination and natural-area parks (Discovery, Woodland, Golden Gardens, Magnuson) stay balanced because officers patrol them; dense-neighborhood playfields (West Queen Anne, Maple Leaf, East Queen Anne, Beacon Hill, Rainier) run 5:1 to 100:1 complaints-over-citations.

Fig 3 · The divergence

Most parks sit above the line

Citations (horizontal) against complaints (vertical), one dot per park with ≥10 combined records (2024–26). The diagonal is parity. Points above it draw more complaints than citations.

Source: PRR C263990 (complaints) vs PRRs C049204 + C263949 (citations), point-in-polygon against the TPL ParkServe Seattle park layer. Parks with ≥10 combined records (n = 66).

What it shows Nearly every park sits above the parity line — complaints outnumber citations almost everywhere, and the gap widens at neighborhood playfields. Only the patrolled destination parks (Discovery, Woodland, Golden Gardens) sit near parity.
