Where the new data could go

Three mockups of how the licensing (PRR C264029) and complaint (PRR C263990) findings could be folded into the existing non-enforcement pages. Each block names its target page, shows a rough component, and notes placement + the caveat that gates it. Numbers are real; layout and copy are drafts to mark up.

Part I · The Gap

Put a real number on “how many dogs are licensed?”

Part I already leans on the “150,000+ dogs” floor. The licensing data lets us state, for the first time, how few of those dogs the city actually has on its books — and that the share is falling. SAS publishes no compliance figure, so this would be ours.

Part II · Access

Show where the dogs are, next to where the OLAs aren’t.

Access maps supply (the 11.7% within a 10-minute walk of an off-leash area). The licensing data adds a demand layer: which neighborhoods have the most dogs. Pairing the two answers “do the dog-dense neighborhoods have access?”

Mock — licensed dogs by neighborhood (2014–25, top 10 ZIPs)

PlacementBest as an overlay/toggle on the existing Access walkshed map (a dog-density layer under the OLA walksheds), or this bar as a supporting exhibit. The strongest version flags dog-dense neighborhoods that fall outside any OLA walkshed — demand vs. supply in one view.
Caveat to keepZIP is the owner’s home address, and counts are 12-year issuance totals, not current or per-capita. Good for “where dogs are owned” at neighborhood scale; normalize by population before ranking “underserved.”
Budget

Dog owners pay far more in licenses than the city spends on off-leash areas.

The Budget page tracks SPR’s small OLA line. The licensing data adds the other side of the ledger: what the city collects from dog owners. The two numbers are an order of magnitude apart.

Mock — annual dog-license revenue vs. OLA-only operating budget

PlacementA single comparison bar or paired stat on the Budget page: “Dog-license revenue ~$1.24M/yr vs. OLA-only operating budget ~$100,000–130,000/yr — the city collects roughly 10× more from dog owners than it spends operating their off-leash areas.”
Caveat that gates thisDifferent departments and funds: license revenue goes to the Seattle Animal Shelter (FAS) for all animal services, not to SPR’s parks budget. This is an honest scale contrast, not a claim that license money should or does fund OLAs. Frame it as a civic observation, with the cross-department note attached.
