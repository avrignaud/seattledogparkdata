# Nextdoor — "Queen Anne Playfield" thread (April 2021)

A ~23-page, 147-comment Nextdoor thread on Seattle's Queen Anne Playfield, launched by a neighbor (Rachel Smith, Upper West Queen Anne) posting a photo of the SPR signage: "DOGS ARE NOT ALLOWED At Play Areas Or Organized Athletic Fields In Seattle Parks. Violations Are Punishable By Fines Of Up To $150. SMC 18.12.080." The thread is the clearest cross-section of neighborhood opinion on the OLA/off-leash question the project has on hand, and it surfaces several data points, arguments, and contacts that belong on-site.

**Source:** PDF capture, archived 2021-04-23. Original thread URL: `https://nextdoor.com/news_feed/?post=184155595` (Nextdoor is a closed platform; the link requires a logged-in Queen Anne neighborhood membership).

**Authorship:** Multiple Nextdoor users across Queen Anne, Magnolia, and adjacent neighborhoods. Names kept as posted; all comments were public to neighborhood members when captured.

---

## Why this thread is valuable

1. **Concrete, neighbor-voiced counterarguments** worth steel-manning on the [opinion page](../docs/opinion.html). Non-dog-owners have real, well-articulated grievances.
2. **Two concrete incidents** at Queen Anne Playfield documented by non-advocates: a small dog reported to have "attacked" an 8-year-old's shoes, and a flag football game that had to be paused multiple times because off-leash dogs kept running into play. Real harm, not hypothetical.
3. **COLA survey data** (via Andre's summary in-thread) on OLA usability — specific amenity counts that aren't yet on the site.
4. **Don Harper** identified as **QACC Parks Chair** with 20 years of advocacy history. A key contact for outreach; he summarizes several attempted OLA sites that were rejected by Parks or by neighbors.
5. **Metropolitan Parks District tax rate**: $0.23 per $1,000 of assessed value ≈ **$53M per year** into the Park District. Allocating even a fraction of that differently would transform OLA funding. Don Harper flagged this in-thread.
6. **Primary-source URLs** cited in-thread: 2018 Park District Annual Report; SPR People, Dogs and Parks Plan executive summary; Seattle Magazine "Seattle's dog obsession."
7. **Mary Cole's testimony** on Kinnear that should be quoted on the site — a non-advocate, non-COLA voice confirming the case study.

## Steel-man: counterarguments to acknowledge honestly

The site's opinion page should be better about treating these as fully legitimate before arguing past them. They are all true in some scope.

### 1. "Dogs are not allowed on this playfield. The rules are clear."
Multiple neighbors (Aaron Baraff, Rachel Smith, Michael Scott, Anna Cashman) repeated this. They are correct. The signage is unambiguous. SMC 18.12.080 is unambiguous. Playfields are dedicated to athletic and organized-play use.

**How the opinion page should treat this:** Agree. Principles P4, P5, and P6 already carry this load. Explicitly name that the thread's core critique — people are breaking a clearly-posted law — is correct. The opinion argues the rules should change, not that they should be ignored.

### 2. "Having a dog is a privilege, not a right."
Michael Scott; Anna Cashman. Agreed in the sense that dog ownership is a discretionary choice, not a civil right.

**How the opinion page should treat this:** The argument for better OLA access is not that dog owners have a constitutional entitlement. It's that when **25% of the city's residents use OLAs** (per SPR's own 2016 survey) and the city allocates **0.06% of the parks budget** to them, the mismatch between constituency and service is a policy failure worth correcting. The counterargument reframes it as a personal-responsibility issue; the site's frame is an urban-policy issue.

### 3. "If you want more OLAs, lobby for them. Don't break the law."
Michael Scott; Cheri Cornell ("If you're mad at the City, why are you taking it out on the people who are using a playfield for its intended purpose?"); Jeffrey Close.

**How the opinion page should treat this:** Partially correct. The opinion page's recommendation is explicitly addressed to the City, not a defense of individual rule-breaking. Andre's own Nextdoor comment acknowledges he personally ignores the rule when playfields are empty. The site should be transparent about that admission and note that acknowledging the current failure is different from endorsing it.

### 4. "Dogs in playfields materially harm other users."
Chris Roozen: "today a small unleashed dog attack the feet of a 8 year old at Big Howe. There is no excuse or reason this should have happened." (10 reactions.) John Barrett: dogs repeatedly ran into a flag football game at Queen Anne Playfield, forcing play to stop. (17 reactions.) Judith Packard: dog feces on shoes. Hildja Saas: athletes rolling in animal feces. Anna Cashman: softball practice where "every practice the girls are stepping in dog poop. It's disgusting."

**How the opinion page should treat this:** These are real and bolster the argument for a *structured* shared-use model over the current shadow-enforcement status quo. A time-zoned model with dedicated cleanup staff addresses exactly these incidents. Saying "dogs disrupt playfields" ≠ saying "the status quo is working" — the current regime produces these harms **despite** the rules, because rules without real enforcement don't prevent behavior. The opinion page's Recommendation #4 ("poop patrol") is a direct response.

### 5. "The ratio of dogs to children isn't a proper metric for how much money the city should spend on dog parks."
Aaron Baraff articulated this carefully: "I don't know that the ratio of dogs to children is any sort of reasonable metric for how much money the city should spend on dog parks vs general use parks. Perhaps it could be used in comparing from city-to-city."

**How the opinion page should treat this:** He's right. "More dogs than kids" is a **scale indicator** showing dog ownership is mainstream in Seattle — not a prescription for proportional funding. The site should say this explicitly where it uses the 153:107 ratio. The site does not, and should not, argue that OLA spending should match playground spending on a per-capita basis.

### 6. "There's no available land — and if there is, it should go to housing or services before dog parks."
Don Harper (20-year advocate): tried MacLean, David Rogers, Smith Cove; rejected or downsized every time. Eric Mandel: "I personally am opposed to converting existing developed city parklands to dog parks." Andre and others across the thread agree on the land constraint.

**How the opinion page should treat this:** Principle P2 already agrees on this point. The opinion should lean harder into the shared-use recommendation and de-emphasize "build more OLAs" as the central ask.

### 7. "What about the people living in their cars and in tents in the park?"
Derwin Hostetler: "NO DOGS, But tents and homeless are ok." Mary Cole: Kinnear's only OLA is "next to the tents full of addicts... I am not going to risk my life for it." Multiple voices, multiple sides of the political spectrum.

**How the opinion page should treat this:** The site already addresses the Kinnear safety issue. It is worth being explicit that the frustration cuts across ideological lines and reflects a general failure of park stewardship — not a dog-owner-vs-everyone-else fight.
