# Part III + Peer-Cities Audit — 2026-04-18

Deep-dive verification of the new content shipped in commit `baf674f` (Part III draft, Austin & Boise peer-city sections, Finding 02b footnote tightening).

## Executive summary
Three SEVERE factual errors, four MODERATE (scope/framing), two MINOR (URLs). The biggest: **Red Bud Isle is 13 acres, not 293** — a claim that appears in `peer-cities.html`, `peer-cities.csv` provenance, AND the project's `CLAUDE.md` memory. It almost certainly propagated from a single original mistake years ago. The 293-acre Austin park is Walnut Creek Metropolitan Park, a different site.

## SEVERE

**S1. Red Bud Isle acreage wrong in three files.**
- `docs/peer-cities.html:529` and `docs/peer-cities.html:534` — "Red Bud Isle (293 ac)" in the Austin stat tile and prose.
- `data/peer-cities.csv:6` — Austin provenance: "excludes Red Bud Isle 293-acre outlier".
- `CLAUDE.md:77` — project-instruction memory: Austin's inflated acreage "is inflated by Red Bud Isle".
- **Actual**: Red Bud Isle is ~13 acres (West Austin Neighborhood Group, multiple sources). The 293-acre figure is **Walnut Creek Metropolitan Park**, a separate site that has an off-leash area.
- Fix: replace with Walnut Creek (or drop the specific-park callout and describe the inflator as "natural-area and voice-control parks"). Update all three locations and add a corrections note.

**S2. NYC press release URL wrong (`?id=19895` → `?id=19877`).**
- `docs/part3.html:348, :360` and `docs/peer-cities.html:493, :515`.
- The correct press release ID for the 2007 off-leash-hours rulemaking is 19877 (confirmed via search; 19895 is a different press release).
- Fix: global replace `?id=19895` → `?id=19877` in both files.

**S3. Westcrest renovation scope overstated.**
- `docs/part3.html`: "rebuilt the OLA's fencing, surface, and drainage with community input..."
- **Actual per [Parkways blog](https://parkways.seattle.gov/2022/06/13/seattle-parks-and-recreation-opens-renovated-and-improved-westcrest-off-leash-area/)**: the $505K scope was drainage, erosion control, access/parking, and accessibility improvements — not fencing. The project was funded from the **Seattle Park District Major Maintenance and Asset Management Fund**, not a capital line. 
- The $505K figure itself IS correct, and HPAC + COLA partnership credits are also correct.
- Fix: rewrite the scope sentence to match the primary source; update "capital funding" → "Major Maintenance fund".

## MODERATE

- Actual: COLA is system-wide; the Parkways Westcrest blog explicitly credits COLA for "ongoing stewardship" of Westcrest too. Genesee-specific framing overstates.
- Fix: describe COLA as system-wide steward with volunteer work at multiple OLAs.

**M7. Boise "18 parks, best-in-class" framing elides seasonal vs. permanent.**
- Per the Boise Parks site: 7 fenced permanent + 11 seasonal / designated-hours = 18.
- If Seattle's methodology (fenced-permanent only) is applied apples-to-apples, Boise is 7, not 18.
- Peer-cities.csv carries 18 without the seasonal disclaimer.
- Fix: add the seasonal-vs-permanent caveat inline and adjust the "best-in-class" framing. The ratio is still very high even at 7/240K (2.92 per 100K), but less than 4× Seattle rather than 4× Seattle.
