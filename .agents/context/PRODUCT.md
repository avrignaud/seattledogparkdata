# PRODUCT.md — Seattle Dog Park Data

> Derived from the project's CLAUDE.md and live site (not a fresh interview). Refine as needed.

**Register:** brand (long-form public-data reference + one signed editorial; design serves credibility and readability, not an app UI).

## What it is
seattledogparkdata.com — a static, sourced, reproducible civic-data reference about Seattle's off-leash dog area (OLA) system: supply, access (walkshed), enforcement, budget, and peer-city comparison. Eight content pages plus a signed opinion editorial. Published from `/docs` as static HTML; no build framework. Every factual claim links to its primary source.

## Users
- Seattle residents and dog owners evaluating the OLA gap.
- Journalists, City Council staff, and policy people who need citable numbers.
- Advocates (e.g., Citizens for Off-Leash Areas) and civic-data readers.
They arrive skeptical and want to trust the numbers fast; many skim, some read deeply and check sources.

## Brand & tone
Facts-first, neutral, restrained, trustworthy. Reads like a credible public-records analysis, not advocacy or marketing. Editorial voice is allowed only on the clearly-marked Opinion page. Quiet confidence; sourcing is the brand. Em-dashes and a measured editorial cadence are intentional house voice.

## Anti-references (what it must NOT look like)
- A SaaS dashboard or "hero-metric" landing template.
- An advocacy/opinion site (except the demarcated Opinion page).
- Clickbait or data-viz spectacle; no tracking, no cookies, no analytics.
- Generic AI-cream startup aesthetic.

## Strategic principles
1. Every factual claim links to a primary source; approximate/derived figures are flagged.
2. Opinion is unmistakably separated from fact.
3. Reproducible: numbers come from committed data + scripts; a verify harness guards key figures.
4. Static, durable, low-maintenance; data refreshes on multi-year timescales.
5. Consistency and legibility over novelty — the reader's trust is the product.
