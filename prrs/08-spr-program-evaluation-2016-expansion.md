# PRR #8 — Program-evaluation, deployment, and decision records for the SPR/FAS park-patrol program ("Making Parks Safer"), 2016–present

**Recipient:** Seattle Parks & Recreation · `PKS_PDR@seattle.gov` (cc: Seattle Animal Shelter / FAS, where appropriate)

**Status:** Filed (awaiting response)

---

## Subject

Requesting program-evaluation, deployment, and decision records for the SPR/FAS Animal Control Officer park-patrol program ("Making Parks Safer"), 2016 to present

## Body

Hello,

I am requesting public records documenting how Seattle Parks & Recreation and the Seattle Animal Shelter / FAS have **evaluated, monitored, and made decisions about** the joint Animal Control Officer + Facilities Maintenance Worker park-patrol program ("Making Parks Safer") since the program was made full-time under the [2016 MOA signed 2016-04-02](../data/moas/SPR-FAS-ACO2-MOA-2016-04-02.pdf) and continued under the [signed 2021 MOA AG21-PRF03-032](../data/moas/SPR-FAS-ACO2-MOA-2021-AG21-PRF03-032.pdf).

The 2019 PRR C049204 returned the citation output of this program (2014 through October 2019). It did not include any of the records that would let a member of the public assess whether the program produced an outcome. I am requesting those records now, specifically:

1. **Internal evaluations, assessments, after-action reviews, or lessons-learned memos** that examine whether the 2016 program expansion changed off-leash compliance, citation patterns, complaint volume, or any other measurable outcome. This includes draft and final documents, whether or not they were ever published externally.

2. **Council, commissioner, or executive briefings and presentations** on the program's effectiveness, prepared by SPR, SAS/FAS, or any contractor, from 2016 to present. Any slide decks, memos, talking points, or response letters.

3. **Patrol deployment logs, dispatch records, or shift summaries** for the ACO II + FMW team, sufficient to show where (which parks, which neighborhoods) the team's hours were spent over the program's life. If detailed dispatch records are voluminous, monthly or quarterly summaries by location are acceptable.

4. **Any compliance, behavior, or complaint-trend analysis** SPR or FAS conducted that goes beyond the raw citation records returned in C049204 — for example, before/after comparisons, modeling of citation rates against complaint rates, or correlation against patrol-hour deployment.

5. **The decision-record for the 2026 staffing expansion** — internal planning documents, memos, council fiscal notes, council Q&A, budget-cycle briefing materials, or executive-team presentations that informed the move from one ACO II (Wednesday–Saturday) to two ACO II positions on seven-day coverage with backup support, as reported in [Axios Seattle on April 17, 2026](https://www.axios.com/local/seattle/2026/04/17/more-paw-patrols-seattle-ramping-up-dog-related-enforcement). What evidence base did SPR/FAS use to justify expanding the staffing model rather than changing it?

6. **Correspondence with community-partner organizations** — Citizens for Off-Leash Areas (COLA), the Magnuson Off-Leash Group (MOLG), neighborhood councils, or similar — that includes program-effectiveness analysis or feedback. Aggregate, redacted, or summary records are fine; I do not need individual constituent contact details.

7. **Any prior public records request, council request, or audit** that asked similar questions to the above and the records released in response. If the 2026 expansion was preceded by an audit (City Auditor, internal compliance review, performance audit), those records are responsive.

If SPR/FAS has not produced any documents in these categories — for example, if the program has run from 2016 to present without an internal effectiveness evaluation — a response confirming that fact is itself responsive. The absence of program-evaluation records is informative for the analysis.

This request is for a public-interest civic-data project at [seattledogparkdata.com](https://seattledogparkdata.com/) analyzing Seattle's OLA system and the cost and outcomes of its enforcement program. Please route to FAS, SAS, the Mayor's Office, or the City Auditor as needed for any records held outside SPR; I am happy to file parallel requests with the appropriate department on your advice.

Thank you,

Andre Vrignaud
seattledogparkdata@ozymandi.as

## Analysis purpose

The factual pages of this site already document the program's **inputs** ([2016 MOA](../data/moas/SPR-FAS-ACO2-MOA-2016-04-02.pdf), [2021 MOA](../data/moas/SPR-FAS-ACO2-MOA-2021-AG21-PRF03-032.pdf), $152,399/yr per ACO II, FAS-side) and its **outputs** (4,803 citations 2014–2019, ~26% cost recovery, 87% first-offense warnings — see [enforcement.html](../docs/enforcement.html)). What is missing is any **outcome** record:

- Did SPR or FAS ever measure whether the 2016 expansion changed off-leash behavior in Seattle parks?
- What compliance trend did SPR observe internally between 2016 and 2026?
- On what evidence base was the 2026 staffing expansion decided?
- Did patrol deployment match citation hotspots, or did it follow a different geographic logic?

Whichever way the answers come back, they let the site replace synthesized observations with documented findings. If the answer is "no such records exist," that is itself a defensible factual claim worth publishing — a $150K-per-year program operated for ten years without an internal evaluation is a meaningful finding about how the program is governed.

## Relationship to other PRRs

- [PRR #1](01-spr-offleash-citations-post-2019.md) extends the citation record set to present. PRR #8 asks for the analysis SPR did *of* that record set.
- [PRR #5](05-spr-ola-usage-counts.md) asks whether SPR measures OLA *use*. PRR #8 asks whether SPR measures patrol-program *effectiveness*. Both are tests of whether the agency tracks outcomes for the systems it funds.
- [PRR #7](07-spr-fas-aco-staffing-expansion.md) asks for the cost and FTE structure of the 2026 expansion. PRR #8 asks for the evidence and decision-making behind it.

Together, the three give a full picture of what SPR/FAS did, what it cost, what it produced, and what — if anything — the agency learned.
