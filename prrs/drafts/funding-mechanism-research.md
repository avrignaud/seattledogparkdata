## The Minneapolis finding

Of the five peers, **Minneapolis is the only one that treats off-leash dog
areas as a user-pays service.** MPRB requires every dog using any of its
eight OLAs to carry an annual off-leash permit — $38/first dog for residents,
$66 for non-residents — and permit revenue is the primary funding source for
OLA operations and maintenance. Closest any US-major-peer comes to a dedicated
revenue model for dog parks.

The other four cities all fund OLAs out of general parks operations with no
dedicated revenue stream.

## Implication for Seattle framing

Seattle's disclosed $100-130K/yr OLA operating line looks sparse next to peer
cities, but **none of the four comparison cities (Portland, SF, Vancouver, DC)
publish a better OLA-specific operating figure either** — they bundle it
inside general parks ops. The honest comparison is on **capital**: Seattle's
$3.1M MPD Cycle 2 sits between DC's ~$1M-per-OLA project spend and Vancouver's
~$1-2M individual OLA builds in the 2023–2026 plan.

What Seattle lacks that SF and Portland have is a **dedicated capital vehicle**
(GO park bonds, Parks Levy). The Metropolitan Park District effectively serves
the same role. What Minneapolis has that **no** peer city has is a dedicated
operating revenue stream via permit fees.
