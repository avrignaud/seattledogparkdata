# Draft research: "What works" OLA profiles

Verified research output (agent ran 19 WebFetches). Use this as source
material for a future Part III or opinion-appendix page. Every claim
below has a URL; review before publishing.

## 1. Magnuson Park OLA (Sand Point) — 8.6 acres

Magnuson is Seattle's largest OLA and the only one with a dedicated
501(c)(3) steward: the **Magnuson Off-Leash Group (MOLG)**, EIN
91-2059268, formed 1999, 501(c)(3) status granted August 2011
([ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/912059268);
[MOLG](https://magnusonolg.org/)). MOLG operates
[magnusonolg.org](https://magnusonolg.org/), a secondary site at
[magnusondogpark.org](https://magnusondogpark.org/), and an active
[Facebook page](https://www.facebook.com/Magnusonoffleashdogpark/).

Mission: partner with Seattle Parks & Recreation on "the health of
park land and the safety and enjoyment of community stakeholders:
canine and human" ([MOLG](https://magnusonolg.org/)). Documented
programs: volunteer work parties, a "Clean Team" that carries extra
waste bags, and fundraising that underwrites on-site bag supplies
and improvement projects.

SPR's own park page credits MOLG with co-funding fencing and signage
([Seattle Parks — Magnuson OLA](https://www.seattle.gov/parks/parks/magnuson-park/off-leash-area)).
MOLG has been the stakeholder partner on the Dog Beach erosion
project; as of October 2023, awaiting a Department of Fish & Wildlife
permit and Army Corps of Engineers approval; stewardship formally
requested ADA paved trail access, additional block steps, and benches
([Magnuson Dog Park — Dog Beach Update, Oct 2023](https://magnusondogpark.org/2023/10/19/dog-beach-update/)).

MOLG files IRS Form 990-N (receipts < $50K), so no detailed revenue
or board roster is public via
[GuideStar](https://www.guidestar.org/profile/91-2059268).

## 2. Genesee Park OLA (Columbia City) — 2.7 acres

Fully-fenced OLA with double-gated entries, a drinking fountain, a
community kiosk, and a gravel center. Small and Shy Dog Area added
Q1 2018 ([Seattle Parks — Genesee](https://www.seattle.gov/parks/recreation/dog-off-leash-areas)).

Managed under the citywide model run by **Citizens for Off-Leash
Areas (COLA)**, the 501(c)(3) that partners with SPR on all OLAs
except Magnuson ([seattlecola.org](https://seattlecola.org/volunteer/)).
Named Genesee steward: **Francine Ruley**, who leads regular work
parties for weeding, gravel spreading, and general maintenance
([Seattle Magazine — *A New Off-Leash Master Plan Has Dog Owners Snarling*](https://seattlemag.com/food-and-culture/new-leash-master-plan-has-dog-owners-snarling/)).

User-run [Genesee Dog Park Group on Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/groups/genesee/)
is the day-to-day social channel. Genesee sits on the historic
Olmsted boulevard system; also covered within
[Friends of Seattle's Olmsted Parks](https://seattleolmsted.org/parks/genesee-park/).

Governance: COLA operates under a cooperative agreement with SPR
that designates per-site stewards; full text not posted publicly
([COLA — About](https://www.seattlecola.info/about-us)).

## 3. Westcrest OLA (Highland Park) — 8.4 acres

Second-largest OLA, with separate small/shy section, training course,
covered shelter, drinking fountain
([Seattle Parks — Westcrest](https://www.seattle.gov/parks/allparks/westcrest-park)).

Managed under COLA. Named steward: **Steve McElhenney**
([seattlecola.org](https://seattlecola.org/volunteer/)).

**Documented investment:** Full renovation closed the OLA from
October 2021 through June 10, 2022. Project cost ~**$505,000**,
funded by the **Seattle Park District Major Maintenance and Asset
Management Fund**, with **HPAC (Highland Park Action Committee)** and
**COLA** credited as partnership and stewardship partners. Scope:
drainage (rock-lined swales, HDPE piping, sediment traps), erosion
control, ADA access, paved pathways, surface restabilization
([Parkways — SPR, June 13, 2022](https://parkways.seattle.gov/2022/06/13/seattle-parks-and-recreation-opens-renovated-and-improved-westcrest-off-leash-area/);
[West Seattle Blog, June 10, 2022](https://westseattleblog.com/2022/06/reopening-day-westcrest-park-off-leash-area-ready-for-use-city-says/);
[West Seattle Blog, Oct 2021 construction start](https://westseattleblog.com/2021/10/followup-long-planned-westcrest-park-work-starting-soon-city-says/)).
SPR also built a temporary OLA on adjacent land during construction
([West Seattle Blog, Oct 2019](https://westseattleblog.com/2019/10/followup-new-plan-for-temporary-off-leash-area-while-westcrest-park-ola-is-closed/)).
