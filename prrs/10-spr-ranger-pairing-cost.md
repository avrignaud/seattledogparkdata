# PRR #10 — SPR-side cost of the Park Ranger positions paired with park-patrol Animal Control Officers, 2023–present

**Recipient:** Seattle Parks & Recreation · `PKS_PDR@seattle.gov`

**Status:** Draft (ready to send)

---

## Subject

Requesting the SPR-side personnel cost and FTE of the Park Ranger (and prior Facilities Maintenance Worker) positions paired with Animal Control Officers under the "Making Parks Safer" park-patrol program, 2023 to present

## Body

Hello,

I am requesting public records documenting the **Seattle Parks & Recreation (SPR)-side cost** of the staff paired with Animal Control Officers (ACOs) on park patrols under the "Making Parks Safer" program.

The SPR / Seattle Animal Shelter Memoranda of Agreement I have on file establish only the Finance & Administrative Services (FAS) side of this program. The [2023 MOA (Project PRF1602)](../data/moas/SPR-FAS-ACO2-MOA-2023-PRF1602.pdf) paired each ACO with an SPR **Facilities Maintenance Worker (FMW)**; the current [2026 MOA](../data/moas/SPR-FAS-ACO2-MOA-2026.pdf) instead pairs each ACO with an SPR **Park Ranger**. Both MOAs state these SPR-side staff are carried on SPR's own books, and neither discloses their cost. I am requesting the records that quantify that SPR-side cost.

Specifically, for fiscal years **2023, 2024, 2025, and 2026**:

1. **The number of SPR FTE assigned to the ACO park-patrol pairing**, by year and position class (Facilities Maintenance Worker and/or Park Ranger), with effective dates — including how many of the two "Maintenance Laborer" positions authorized in the 2023 Park District Cycle 2 "Park Safety Program" proposal ($448,640) were actually filled, and when the pairing transitioned from FMW to Park Ranger.

2. **The fully-loaded annual personnel cost** (salary + benefits + overhead) for each SPR-side paired position assigned to the program, by year — the SPR-side analogue of the FAS Attachment A / cost-summary table in the MOAs.

3. **The budget line items** (BSL / program / project / ledger codes) that fund these SPR-side positions, so the SPR-side commitment is traceable in the adopted budget books alongside the FAS-side Project PRF1602.

4. **Any allocation or methodology** SPR uses to attribute Park Ranger time to the ACO pairing specifically (as distinct from general park-ranger duties), if one exists. If Park Rangers paired with ACOs are drawn from the general ranger corps with no separate cost line, a response confirming that — and stating how SPR would estimate the attributable share — is itself responsive.

This request is for a public-interest civic-data project at [seattledogparkdata.com](https://seattledogparkdata.com/) analyzing Seattle's off-leash area system and the cost and deployment of its enforcement program.

Thank you,

Andre Vrignaud
seattledogparkdata@ozymandi.as

## Analysis purpose

The site documents the **FAS-side** cost of the park-patrol ACO program precisely — $454,652/yr (2023 MOA), re-priced to $528,279/yr (2026 MOA), authorized for three ACO IIs. What it cannot yet state with primary-source backing is the **SPR-side** cost of the paired staff, which the [Opinion page](../docs/opinion.html#O1) previously estimated by scaling a ~$140,000/yr FMW figure (author estimate). Two facts now make that estimate unreliable:

1. The 2026 MOA changed the paired role from a dedicated FMW to an **existing Park Ranger** drawn from the general ranger corps — a different (and not separately published) cost basis.
2. The 2023 Park District budget book's "Park Safety Program" package ($448,640 for two new ACOs **and** two new Maintenance Laborers) implies a per-laborer cost closer to **~$73,000** than the ~$140,000 author estimate — though that subtraction is fragile (it depends on whether the ACOs were costed as partial-year hires).

This request would replace the scaled all-in estimate with a documented SPR-side number, letting the site state the program's total cost — not just the FAS half — with primary-source backing. It complements [PRR #9](09-spr-aco-monthly-parks-reports.md) (which targets *deployment*: how much funded capacity actually reaches park patrols).
