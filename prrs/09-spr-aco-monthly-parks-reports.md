## Subject

Requesting the monthly parks-patrol summary reports and cost-calculation spreadsheets that the current SPR/FAS Animal Control Officer MOA (Project PRF1602, signed 2023) requires the departments to produce, 2023 to present

## Body

Hello,

I am requesting public records that the **current SPR / Seattle Animal Shelter Memorandum of Agreement** for Animal Control Officer park patrols requires the departments to generate on a recurring basis. That agreement — the [signed 2023 MOA, Project PRF1602](../data/moas/SPR-FAS-ACO2-MOA-2023-PRF1602.pdf), which funds three ACO II positions under the "Making Parks Safer" initiative — obligates the departments to produce two recurring records that have not previously been requested. I received the MOA itself via PRR C265341-042926; this request seeks the operational records it calls for.

Specifically:

1. **The monthly parks-data summary reports described in §5 of the MOA**, for every month from the agreement's effective date (2023) to present. Section 5 states that "within five working days of the end of each month, SPR will provide FAS a summary report on that month's data, which FAS will include with its invoice to SPR Accounting." Per §5, that data includes the origin and type of each interaction with a member of the public, the infraction observed, the result of the interaction (e.g., patrol and incident; scoop violation; warning), the location and time of each interaction, and each visit by the team to a single park. I am requesting these monthly reports as delivered.

2. **The underlying "Parks Code Violation Dashboard" records** referenced in §5, for the ACO/FMW park-patrol team, 2023 to present, if they can be exported as a dataset (CSV or equivalent). Section 5 states the team records all contacts and citations in "the SPR app on the Parks Code Violation Dashboard." A per-record or per-park, per-month export is preferred; if that is voluminous, monthly or quarterly summaries by park are acceptable.

3. **The cost-calculation spreadsheets described in §7 of the MOA**, for every billing period from 2023 to present. Section 7 states that FAS submits monthly payment requests to SPR via a pre-established interdepartmental journal entry, "along with a calculation spreadsheet of these costs." These spreadsheets would show the actual hours billed and amounts charged per pay period — i.e., the actual, as-billed staffing level over time, as distinct from the authorized three-FTE figure in the MOA.

4. **Any monthly or quarterly rollup** SPR or FAS maintains of (a) the number of parks patrolled, (b) the officer-hours spent on park patrols, and (c) the share of ACO time spent on parks versus other field-service duties, 2023 to present. If no such rollup is maintained separately from the §5 reports above, a response confirming that is itself responsive.

If any of these records are held by FAS or the Seattle Animal Shelter rather than SPR, please route accordingly and advise so I can file a parallel request; the §5 reports originate with SPR but are delivered to and retained by FAS, and the §7 spreadsheets originate with FAS.

This request is for a public-interest civic-data project at [seattledogparkdata.com](https://seattledogparkdata.com/) analyzing Seattle's off-leash area system and the cost and deployment of its enforcement program.

Thank you,

Andre Vrignaud
seattledogparkdata@ozymandi.as

## Analysis purpose

PRR C265341 established what the program **costs** and **is authorized at**: the 2023 MOA funds three ACO II positions at $454,652/year (FAS-side), up from one. What it did not establish is how much of that funded capacity actually reaches park patrols — let alone off-leash enforcement specifically. The [enforcement page](../docs/enforcement.html#finding-02) makes this gap explicit (the "funded vs. what reaches off-leash enforcement" chart) and deliberately holds its cost-per-citation denominator conservative because the deployment is undisclosed.

The §5 monthly reports and §7 spreadsheets are the records that would close that gap:

- **§7 spreadsheets** show the *actually-billed* hours per pay period — the real, filled FTE by year, which the MOA's authorized "3 FTE" figure does not guarantee (the 2022 SLI and March 2023 correspondence show the two new positions were still being hired mid-2023).
- **§5 reports** show *where* the team actually went — parks patrolled, contacts made, results (warning vs. citation) — which would let the site state, with primary-source backing, how much of the tripled funding lands on park patrols and how the contact-to-citation ratio has moved since the authority gap (officers' inability to compel ID) was documented in 2022.

Either way the answer is informative. If the reports show patrols scaled with funding, the deployment question is resolved. If they show flat or declining park presence despite three funded officers, that is a direct, documented measure of the funded-vs-deployed gap.
