# PRR #4 — Methodology behind SPR's 2.5-mile OLA access standard

**Recipient:** Seattle Parks & Recreation · `PKS_PDR@seattle.gov`

**Status:** Filed (awaiting response)

---

## Subject

Requesting methodology documentation for SPR's OLA access standard cited in the People, Dogs and Parks Strategic Plan

## Body

Hello,

SPR's 2017 *People, Dogs and Parks Strategic Plan* defends the current off-leash area inventory in part by noting that most Seattle residents are within approximately 2.5 miles of an OLA. I am requesting the underlying methodology and source data used to produce that statement. Specifically:

1. **The exact distance standard used** (2.5 miles straight-line, 2.5 miles along road network, a travel-time equivalent, or other), and the reasoning behind selecting it.
2. **The population dataset used** (U.S. Census year and geography — block group, tract, etc.).
3. **Whether the analysis accounts for physical barriers** — Interstate 5, State Route 99, the Lake Washington Ship Canal, freeway on/off-ramps, rail corridors, topography, bridges — and if so, which.
4. **The tool, software, or contractor** that produced the analysis, including any geospatial files (shapefiles, GeoJSON, service areas) generated during the study.
5. **The resulting coverage percentage** (what share of Seattle residents are within the standard, by the Plan's own calculation) and any demographic breakdowns produced (by race, income, age, or neighborhood).
6. **Any subsequent revision, update, or reassessment** of this access standard, including work produced for the 2023–2024 OLA Expansion Study.

If analogous methodology documentation exists for the 2023–2024 Expansion Study's coverage analysis, I would like that included as well.

This data will be used for a public-interest civic-data project at seattledogparkdata.com. The project compares SPR's OLA access standard to Trust for Public Land's ParkScore industry standard of a 0.5-mile / 10-minute walk using a network-distance analysis, and we are working to present both methodologies transparently and side-by-side.

Thank you,

Andre Vrignaud
seattledogparkdata@ozymandi.as

## Analysis purpose

Either outcome is useful:
- **If SPR has a rigorous methodology**: compare it apples-to-apples with TPL's 0.5-mile network walkshed and present the results side-by-side. This gives readers a direct view of how the standard chosen shapes the conclusion.
- **If SPR's methodology is informal or undocumented**: that itself is reportable — a coverage claim without reproducible methodology isn't a defense of the status quo.
